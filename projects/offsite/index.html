01 / The scene becomes the training ground

One walkthrough.
Many places to act.

A captured room carries more than its appearance. It supplies the geometry a mobile robot must navigate and the surfaces its arms must interact with.

OffSite turns that capture into a reusable environment for generating demonstrations, with different task sites, object placements, and robot starting configurations.

02 / How it works

Capture. Verify. Learn.

Appearance and contact geometry come from the same reconstruction, so camera views and physical interactions share one coordinate frame.

  1. 01

    Reconstruct the scene

    A 3D Gaussian Splatting model supplies scene appearance. A mesh from the same reconstruction supplies contact and collision geometry.

  2. 02

    Generate verified rollouts

    Sample task layouts, execute grasp and base motions in simulation, and keep only collision-free, reachable, successful episodes. Vision feedback refines grasp parameters after failures.

  3. 03

    Train, then deploy

    Depth compositing creates head- and wrist-camera training views. A vision-language-action policy learns from those views, robot state, and instructions, then runs closed loop on the real robot.

03 / Real-robot evidence

What the result depends on

Capture the place
the robot will work in.

The walkthrough must cover the viewpoints the robot will occupy. Missing views or geometry can reduce the yield of usable demonstrations, and substantial changes to the scene require a fresh capture.

The evaluation covers one robot platform and four rigid-object tasks. Deformable manipulation, tactile dexterity, and transfer across robot embodiments are outside this evaluation.
